**Compiled:** 14 Nov 2026
**Purpose:** show, with real forms and steps, why the current process is slow and fragmented.
**Important:** the *routes, steps and forms* below come from the cited sources. The **friction analysis** and the **count of channels** are **our own synthesis**, not published statistics. Label them that way on slides.

## 2. Our synthesis (label as analysis on slides)

| Observation | Basis |
|---|---|
| A hotel with banquet surplus may have **up to nine distinct routes or obligations** to choose between or satisfy (R1 to R9), each with its own contact point, form or account. | Count of rows above. This is our count, not a published figure. |
| **Most routes are contact-based** (phone, email, "request an assessment"), not instant. | R1, R2, R3 |
| **The one permit with a published lead time (about 15 days)** is far longer than the hours in which cooked surplus stays safe. Note: the IACAD permit applies to people distributing meals, so check whether it applies to hotel-to-charity donations. | R4, C15 |
| **Composting and rescue are separate relationships**, even though a Food Bank and ne'ma programme already links them in campaigns. | R1, R2, D08, D13 |
| **Evidence for DET is the hotel's own job.** DET lists a food-waste requirement with no template, so the hotel builds its own records. | R8, G01 |
| **Who decides is unclear at midnight.** No source describes a single owner for the decision. | Our reasoning, to test in survey and mentor sessions |
